# NASA STI document requests — FPS array-processor material

Drafted 2026-07-25 from the NTRS sweep in
`refs_extracted/ntrs_array_processor_sources.md`.

Two separate asks, in priority order. **Submit them as two separate
inquiries** — the form takes one Document ID per submission, and they
are different problems (missing pages in a held document vs. a document
with no file at all).

## Where to send

- **Preferred:** NASA Document Inquiry Form —
  <https://sti.nasa.gov/doc-inquiry/>
- **Alternative:** email the STI Information Desk at `help@sti.nasa.gov`
  (phone 443-757-5802). Use the email body in §3 below.
- Response time is stated as ~5 business days; all STI items are free.

Form fields, for reference: First Name, Last Name, Email, Affiliation
(dropdown — use **General Interest**), Document ID, Title, and a free-text
"Questions, Comments, or Feedback" box. Fill in your own name; the
Document ID / Title / body text are given below verbatim.

---

## 1. Missing figure plates in NASA-CR-171283 (highest value)

**Document ID:** `19850008016`
(report numbers NASA-CR-171283 / FR1021 / NAS 1.26:171283)

**Title:** Atmospheric Modeling And Sensor Simulation (AMASS) study

**Questions, Comments, or Feedback:**

> The PDF available for this record appears to be missing several figure
> plates. Section IV ("Technical Characteristics") refers to Figure 3
> (architecture of the AP-120B), Figure 4 (FPS-5000 series general
> architecture design), and Figure 5 (FPS-5000 system and
> architecture), but no pages carrying those figures are present in the
> file. The printed page numbers run continuously — 18, 19, 20 (the
> Section IV text that cites Figures 3, 4 and 5), 21 (Figure 6, CSPI
> MAP-400), 22, 23 (Figure 7, Analogic AP500), 24 — so the plates for
> Figures 2 through 5 seem to have been dropped when the document was
> filmed or scanned, rather than being misordered within the file. The
> surviving plates from that series are Figure 1 and Figures 6 through
> 12.
>
> Could the original paper or microform copy be checked for those pages,
> and a rescan of the missing plates supplied if they exist?
>
> The Figure 4 and Figure 5 diagrams are of particular interest to me.
> The FPS-5000 was a commercial array processor whose manufacturer,
> Floating Point Systems Inc. of Beaverton, Oregon, went out of business
> in 1991, and essentially no technical documentation for that
> particular product line survives in public archives. I am working on a
> volunteer effort to restore and document a surviving FPS-3000 machine
> from that same product family, and this report's independent 1984
> description of the architecture is one of the very few contemporaneous
> sources on it that exists anywhere. Any form the pages can be supplied
> in would be helpful, including a plain photograph of the paper copy.

## 2. No full text attached for the 1985 AMASS extension study

**Document ID:** `19870003657`

**Title:** Atmospheric Modeling And Sensor Simulation (AMASS) study

**Questions, Comments, or Feedback:**

> This record is marked as PUBLIC distribution but has no full text
> attached and gives no external online source. The abstract describes
> an extension study of off-the-shelf array processors interfacing to a
> Perkin-Elmer 3250 host, including the evaluation of a Floating Point
> Systems AP-120B borrowed from another Marshall Space Flight Center
> laboratory.
>
> Is a scan of the full text available, or can one be made? This appears
> to be the follow-on to NASA-CR-171283 (record 19850008016, contract
> NAS8-35189, New Technology Inc.), whose corresponding array-processor
> evaluation section I have found useful.
>
> For context: I am part of a volunteer effort documenting surviving
> Floating Point Systems array-processor hardware. The manufacturer
> closed in 1991 and almost no technical documentation for these
> machines survives publicly, so NASA-era evaluation reports have become
> unusually important secondary sources. Any level of scan quality would
> be useful.

## Lower-priority follow-ups (not drafted)

Worth a separate inquiry only if the two above land well. Both are
citation-only records with no file, and both are journal or conference
reprints, so STI may hold no distributable copy at all:

- **`19820055680`** — Wu, Barkan, Karplus, Caswell (JPL), *Seasat
  synthetic-aperture radar data reduction using parallel programmable
  array processors*, 1982, contract NAS7-100. SEL 32/77 host driving
  three AP-120B units. Filed as a REPRINT, so the journal publisher
  likely holds the rights — probably easier to obtain through normal
  literature channels than from STI.
- **`19840017832`** — *Demodulator and Accumulator for the High-speed
  Data Acquisition System*, 1984. Mentions a "floating point systems
  **5210** array processor", a part number we have not seen elsewhere.
